Topic 1

1. Recognizing and Reporting Phishing

The Threat: Cybercriminals send deceptive emails or messages disguised as university admin or staff to steal your credentials.

Why You Should Care: A single compromised university account can lead to identity theft and unauthorized access to personal and campus records.

Behavior Change: Stop clicking suspicious links immediately. Inspect sender addresses carefully and verify unexpected requests.

CISA Recommendation: Recognize common phishing indicators (urgent language, mismatched URLs) and report them to IT support right away.

Real-World Scenario: A student receives an email titled "Urgent: Financial Aid Update" asking them to log in via a link. Instead of clicking, the student checks the sender address, realizes it's fake, and reports it.

Topic 2

2. Strong Passwords & Password Managers

The Threat: Weak or reused passwords allow attackers to compromise multiple personal and academic accounts easily.

Why You Should Care: Reusing your university password across entertainment or social media sites puts all your accounts at risk if one site is breached.

Behavior Change: Use long, unique passphrases for every single account and stop reusing old passwords.

CISA Recommendation: Use passwords that are at least 16 characters long or use a secure password manager to generate and store them.

Topic 3

3. Enabling Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

The Threat: Passwords alone are no longer enough to stop unauthorized access.

Why You Should Care: MFA adds a critical extra layer of defense, making it significantly harder for hackers to access your account even if they know your password.

Behavior Change: Turn on MFA across all personal, financial, and university accounts wherever available.

CISA Recommendation: Enable MFA using authenticator apps or security keys rather than basic SMS codes when possible.
